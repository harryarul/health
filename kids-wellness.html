The toolkit

Exercise library

Here is every exercise the plan draws from, with a Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced version. The 14-day calendar tells you which to do each day.

🧠

Focus & Study

Attention is a muscle. Short, repeated reps grow it — and a calm body makes a focused mind.

Beginner

Box breathing for 1 min, then stare at one object naming 5 details. Play one round of Memory Match.

Intermediate

15-min Focus Timer study sprint on real homework. After, recall 3 facts out loud without looking.

Advanced

25-min deep sprint + "teach-back": explain what you learned aloud as if teaching a younger kid.

🤝

Social Connection

Confidence with people is a skill you practise, not a trait you're born with. Small daily reps add up fast.

Beginner

Make warm eye contact and greet one person by name. Ask one "what" or "how" question and listen.

Intermediate

Active listening: repeat back what a friend said in your own words. Give two genuine compliments today.

Advanced

Start or lead a group game at recess, and tell a 2-minute story about your day at dinner.

⚽

Sport & Energy

Movement that crosses the body's midline (left↔right) lights up both brain sides — great for energy and focus.

Beginner

30s toe-taps on the ball, jog in place, 10 ball juggles attempts, basic side-to-side shuffles.

Intermediate

Dribble through 5 cones, aim for 20 juggles, 4×20m sprints, ladder/agility footwork.

Advanced

Circuit: juggle → sprint → shoot, repeat 4 rounds. Finish with a 6-minute steady stamina run.

🎵

Music & Rhythm

Keeping a beat trains timing, prediction and patience — the same brain skills that help with reading and maths.

Beginner

Clap a steady beat with the metronome at 90 BPM. Hum a favourite tune all the way through.

Intermediate

Clap an off-beat rhythm, then learn a short phrase of a song on voice or any instrument.

Advanced

Play or sing a full short piece, improvise 8 bars of rhythm, then perform it for one person.

A note for grown-ups 💛

This is a fun wellness routine, not a medical or therapy program. Every child develops at their own pace — celebrate effort over perfection, and let your child skip or repeat days as needed. If you have ongoing concerns about attention, mood, social development, or energy, it's worth chatting with your child's doctor or a school counsellor, who can give advice tailored to your child. Always supervise physical activity and make sure your child warms up, stays hydrated, and stops if anything hurts.

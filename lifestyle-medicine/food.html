Eat

Eat like it's treatment.

No single "superfood" saves you. A consistent pattern — more whole plants, far less ultra-processed food — does the heavy lifting, and the effect on cholesterol, blood sugar and weight rivals the early reach of medication.

THE FOUNDATIONBuild the plate from plants

Properly planned, plant foods supply everything the body needs, with high nutritional density. Make these the base of most meals:

🥬

Leafy greens

Kale, spinach, collards — calcium, iron, and vitamins A, C and K.

🫘

Legumes

Beans, lentils, peas — protein, fibre, iron and zinc in one package.

🌰

Nuts & seeds

Healthy fats, protein, plus selenium and magnesium.

🌾

Whole grains

B vitamins, fibre, iron and zinc — choose whole over refined.

One genuine watch-point: if you eat fully plant-based, vitamin B12 is mainly found in animal foods, so use fortified plant milks or a supplement. Easy to cover once you know.

CHOLESTEROLFood that lowers LDL

Across 30 randomised trials, vegetarian and vegan diets cut total cholesterol by about 7%, LDL by about 10%, and apolipoprotein B by about 14% — roughly a third of what a statin achieves, from eating alone.

  • Oats & whole grains — soluble fibre that blocks cholesterol absorption
  • Legumes — fibre and protein that lower LDL
  • Nuts — heart-healthy fats and plant sterols
  • Fruit & vegetables — pectin and other cholesterol-binding fibres
  • Soy foods — may lower total and LDL cholesterol

The amplifiers: limit saturated fat, eliminate trans fats, and lean into soluble fibre and plant sterols. Individual responses vary — if you take a cholesterol medicine, track your numbers with your doctor as you change your diet.

PROTECTIONAntioxidants — from food, not pills

Vitamin C and E, selenium and lycopene help neutralise the free radicals that damage blood vessels. But here's the catch: large trials of antioxidant supplements have been disappointing. The benefit shows up when these compounds arrive together, in whole fruit, vegetables, nuts and grains — fibre and other nutrients included.

Eat the tomato, not the lycopene capsule.

THE OTHER HALFTreat ultra-processed food like tobacco

Cardiologist Dr Aseem Malhotra argues the biggest dietary win isn't a new pill — it's pulling ultra-processed food out of daily life. His public-health framework rests on three levers:

A

Availability

Less junk food in schools, hospitals and everyday default settings.

A

Acceptability

Shift how we see ultra-processed snacks — clear front-of-pack warnings, fewer ads.

A

Affordability

Use pricing — like the UK's 2018 sugar levy — to nudge choices and push reformulation.

The UK sugar levy is the proof of concept: it cut added-sugar intake and prompted manufacturers to reformulate. The science on exactly which processed foods drive which diseases is still being argued, but the direction — more whole food, less ultra-processed — is one almost everyone agrees on.

!

Big dietary changes can interact with medication.

If you take medicines for diabetes, blood pressure, cholesterol, or blood thinning (warfarin is sensitive to vitamin-K-rich greens), tell your doctor before overhauling your diet — doses sometimes need adjusting. That's a good problem to have, and one to manage together.

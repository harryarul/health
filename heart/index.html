Pillar one — Movement

A weekly rhythm
your heart will thank you for.

You don't need a gym. A simple weekly structure of aerobic work, strength, and recovery does the heavy lifting.

Aerobic base

150 min

Moderate activity per week — brisk walking, cycling, swimming. About 30 min, 5 days.

Strength

2 days

Muscle-strengthening sessions weekly. Bodyweight, bands, or weights — all major muscle groups.

Mobility & calm

10 min

Daily stretching, yoga, or breathing. Lowers stress hormones and supports recovery.

Move often

8k steps

Break up sitting. Frequent light movement matters as much as one big workout.

A friendly target many guidelines point to: at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity (or 75 minutes vigorous) plus 2 strength sessions each week. Start where you are and build gradually — consistency beats intensity.

Your 14-day movement plan

Pick the level that matches you today. You can always start lower and graduate up.

Always warm up for a few minutes and cool down. Rest if you feel chest pain, dizziness, or unusual breathlessness, and don't train through illness — these are signs to stop and, if they persist, to see a doctor.

Pillar two — Food as medicine

Every meal is a
small dose of prevention.

A Mediterranean-style way of eating is the most studied pattern for the heart: plants first, healthy fats, fish over red meat, and very little ultra-processed food.

  • Fill half the plate with plantsVegetables and fruit at every meal — color and fiber are the goal.
  • Choose healthy fatsOlive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado, and oily fish instead of butter and fried foods.
  • Go whole grainOats, brown rice, quinoa, whole wheat — slower energy, more fiber.
  • Cut the salt & added sugarSeason with herbs, lemon, garlic, and spices. Watch hidden salt in processed food.
  • Fish & legumes over red meatAim for oily fish twice a week; beans and lentils for plant protein.

How to read your own lab report

When the results come back, they can look like a wall of numbers and arrows. Here's how to make sense of yours in six quick steps — so the conversation with your doctor is a partnership, not a mystery.

1
Find the right lines

Your lipid panel lists Total, LDL, HDL and Triglycerides. Blood pressure shows two numbers — systolic over diastolic, e.g. 118/76.

2
Check the units

The US reports cholesterol in mg/dL; much of the world uses mmol/L. Rough cholesterol conversion: divide mg/dL by ~38.7. For triglycerides, divide by ~88.5. So LDL 100 mg/dL ≈ 2.6 mmol/L.

3
Read the reference range

Each report prints the lab's own "normal" range beside your result, often with an H (high) or L (low) flag. Use it as a guide — but your personal targets depend on your overall risk.

4
Look beyond the basics

Two useful extras: non-HDL (Total minus HDL) and the Total ÷ HDL ratio. Your doctor may also order newer markers like ApoB or Lp(a) for a sharper risk picture.

5
Track the trend

One reading is a snapshot; the trend is the story. Keep a simple log over months. For blood pressure, take several calm, seated readings rather than trusting a single number.

6
Bring questions, not conclusions

Note anything flagged and ask: "What's driving this, and what's my target?" Don't change or stop medication on your own — that's a decision to make together with your clinician.

🧘

Stress & calm

Chronic stress keeps blood pressure and inflammation elevated. A few daily minutes of slow breathing, a walk outdoors, or time away from screens measurably helps. Aim for one genuine wind-down ritual a day.

😴

Sleep

During deep sleep, blood pressure and heart rate dip and recover. Consistently short or broken sleep pushes both numbers the wrong way. Target 7–9 hours, a steady schedule, and a cool, dark room.

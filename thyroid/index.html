🦋 Pillar · Thyroid

Care for your thyroid.

A small, butterfly-shaped gland sets the pace of your whole metabolism. When it slows down, everything feels harder. Here's how to spot it early and support it — naturally first, with your doctor alongside.

🦋

What the thyroid actually does.

It produces hormones (T3 and T4) that tell nearly every cell how fast to work — your energy, temperature, heart rate, digestion, weight and mood all answer to it. When it underperforms, your pituitary raises a signal called TSH to push it harder. A high TSH with still-normal T3/T4 means your body is compensating — an early, very workable stage often caused by Hashimoto's, an autoimmune condition.

Listen to your body

Quiet signs it may be slowing down.

None of these alone means much — but a cluster, especially with a family history, is worth a blood test.

🔋

Energy & body

  • Persistent fatigue
  • Feeling cold when others don't
  • Weight creeping up unexplained
  • Dry skin, thinning hair
🧠

Mind & mood

  • Brain fog, poor focus
  • Low mood or flatness
  • Sleeping a lot but unrefreshed
⚙️

Inner systems

  • Constipation
  • Slower heart rate
  • Heavier or irregular periods
  • Rising cholesterol
Step 1 · Know your numbers

Start with a blood test.

You can't tell what's helping without a starting line. Ask your doctor for the full picture, not just TSH on its own.

🩸

Thyroid panel

  • TSH — the master signal
  • Free T4 — storage hormone
  • Free T3 — active hormone
  • Anti-TPO antibodies — confirms Hashimoto's
🧪

Supporting labs

  • Vitamin D — aim 50–80 ng/mL
  • Ferritin — iron stores
  • Vitamin B12
  • Cholesterol panel
Step 2 · Food first

Eat to calm the system.

An anti-inflammatory, nutrient-dense plate gives the gland what it needs and quiets the immune attack behind Hashimoto's.

🥗

Eat liberally

  • Protein every meal — eggs, fish, poultry, beans
  • Cooked veg — sweet potato, squash, greens
  • Healthy fats — olive oil, avocado, nuts
  • Fermented foods daily for gut health
  • Berries & whole fruit
🌰

The hero: selenium

  • 1–2 Brazil nuts a day (~100–200 mcg)
  • Studies show it can lower thyroid antibodies
  • Never exceed 400 mcg/day
Good evidencePennies a day
🚫

Try removing

  • Gluten — fully, for 12 weeks. The biggest dietary lever in Hashimoto's.
  • Ultra-processed food & added sugar
  • Most alcohol
Step 3 · Gentle support

Modest doses, real nutrients.

💊

A simple daily stack

  • Vitamin D3 2,000–4,000 IU (with K2)
  • Magnesium glycinate 200–400 mg at night
  • Zinc 15–30 mg with food
  • Omega-3 1–2 g
  • Probiotic — multi-strain
⚠️

Be careful with these

  • Iodine, kelp, high-dose seaweed — in Hashimoto's these can worsen the immune attack. Even Dr. Axe's own site warns high-dose iodine aggravates thyroid symptoms.
  • Desiccated thyroid (NDT) — only with a doctor.
  • Megadoses of anything. More isn't better.
Step 4 · The quiet basics

Sleep, calm, and gentle movement.

Chronic stress raises cortisol, which blunts the conversion of T4 into active T3. The boring habits move the needle most.

😴

Sleep

  • 7–9 hours, same bedtime
  • Dark, cool room
  • Morning sunlight early
🚶

Movement

  • 30+ min walking daily
  • Strength work 2–3×/week
  • Don't over-train — it backfires
🧘

Calm

  • 10 min daily — breath, prayer, journaling
  • Fewer doom-scroll inputs
  • Filtered water, cleaner products
Step 5 · Let the labs decide

Retest on a schedule.

Thyroid hormones shift slowly. Give your changes a full 12 weeks before judging — and let your numbers, not your feelings, make the call.

Reading week 12

Three honest outcomes.

Keep going

TSH falling toward 4.5, antibodies down, feeling better

It's working. Hold steady through week 24 — don't add anything new.

Hold & reassess

TSH steady, but you feel noticeably better

Fair to continue another 12 weeks. Check you've really stuck to it, then re-decide.

Time to act

TSH past 10, climbing, or symptoms worse

Lifestyle alone isn't enough here. Talk to your doctor about low-dose medication — natural and medical aren't enemies, and untreated low thyroid has real costs.

Don't wait

See your doctor now if…

  • Severe fatigue you can't push through
  • Big unexplained weight gain
  • Swelling in face, hands or legs; slow heartbeat
  • Depression that's deepening
  • Pregnant or trying to conceive — untreated low thyroid is genuinely risky for the baby. Non-negotiable.
  • Trouble swallowing or a visible lump (goiter)
  • Heart racing, heat intolerance — Hashimoto's can swing the other way
